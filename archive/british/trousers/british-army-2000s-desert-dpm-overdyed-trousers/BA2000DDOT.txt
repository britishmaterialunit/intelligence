British Army 2000s Desert DPM Overdyed Trousers

Nation: British
Type: Trousers
Archive ref: BA2000DDOT

The British Army 2000s Desert DPM Overdyed Trousers are cut from lightweight cotton.

They carry British Desert DPM camouflage taken down by an overdye. The four-colour
desert print is still legible in the cloth but the sand and stone have gone to a
flat charcoal, so what is left is the shape of the pattern rather than its colour —
the brushstrokes reading as a tonal figure against ground.

They have an elasticated waist with an internal drawcord, elasticated gathered
ankle cuffs, a straight full-cut leg and a plain front with no belt loops.

Desert DPM was the British Army's arid-theatre print from the first Gulf War to the
introduction of MTP, and trousers in this cut were issued as a lightweight layer to
be worn over or instead of combat trousers. The overdye is a later hand: surplus
put through a vat so it can be worn as clothing rather than as uniform.
